# SolfaScores — handoff for the next agent

SolfaScores is an app for choirs and music groups that read **tonic sol-fa**. Members learn their part by hearing it played, and leaders organise songs, services and rehearsals in one place. The first users are church choirs in Nigeria (Catholic, Anglican, Pentecostal), plus non-church groups such as bands and orchestras. It will go international later.

The product owner has a pilot choir ready to test. The real app is started: `abu-app/` (the Expo app: sol-fa reader, score and player, live on the web) and `abu-api/` (the Cloudflare Worker: website hosting, API and D1 database). **Next: the owner's pilot steps (the Android test build on Tecno, Infinix and itel phones, and onboarding the pilot choir; see "Pilot ready" in `docs/app-plan.md`), then the order of work after the MVP.**

## What's in the repository

- **`prototype/index.html`:** the clickable prototype, one self-contained file. It's the best spec for screens, flows, wording and behaviour. Open it in a browser. Its `seed()` function holds example data and four personas:
  - Adaeze, an alto member
  - Chiamaka, the soprano part leader
  - Emeka, the choirmaster
  - Tobi, an individual with no group

  Parts worth porting rather than rewriting:
  - **`parseSolfa(text, time)`:** the sol-fa reader. It handles sections, part lines, word lines, `:` beats, `/` half-bar marks, `|` barlines, `.` half beats, `,` quarter beats, `-` holds, rests, octave marks (`d'`, `d¹`, `t1`, `t₁`) and chromatic syllables. It returns note events in beats plus readable errors such as "Tenor, bar 2 has 5 beats; 4/4 needs 4." Port it to TypeScript as a pure module and **unit-test it first**.
  - **`scoreHTML()`:** lays out the score in columns, one per beat, with lines balanced so no single bar is left alone. Use it as the reference for the React score component.
  - **Audio engine** (`voice`, `tick`, `startPlay`, `partRef`, `doh`): Web Audio synth with per-part gain for mute, solo and "focus my part" (others at 28%), tempo changes while playing, transpose and loop. On native, `react-native-audio-api` (Software Mansion) offers a Web Audio-style API, so the engine can be shared across web and phones.
  - **Write-with-pen screen** (`SCREENS.write`, `wDown`, `wMove`, `wUp`, `writeSolfa`): ink with pen pressure, palm rejection once a stylus is seen, eraser, undo and the Fix keypad. Recognition there is **simulated** against a demo hymn, so the real version needs an actual recogniser.
- **`docs/solfa-format.md`:** the sol-fa text format spec, based on real choir sheets. Treat it as the source of truth for the parser.
- **`docs/app-plan.md`:** the agreed plan for the whole app: the five tabs, onboarding, what each tab contains, roles, look and feel, Nigeria-first details, and the order of work after the MVP.
- **`design/brand/`:** the master SVGs, with every letter drawn as a shape so they need no fonts: `icon.svg` (the app icon, 1024 square) and `mark.svg` (the mark alone, for red backgrounds). The app's icon files are exported from them:
  - `abu-app/assets/images/`: `icon.png` (1024, no transparency), `android-icon-foreground.png` and `android-icon-monochrome.png` (1024, the mark 480 wide on nothing, inside Android's round safe zone; `android-icon-background.png` is the red with rings), `splash-icon.png` (the mark, 1240 wide; `imageWidth` 140 in `app.json`) and `favicon.png` (96, rounded).
  - `abu-app/public/icons/`: `icon-512.png`, `icon-192.png`, `maskable-512.png` and `apple-touch-icon.png` (180), all without transparency.
  - In code, `BrandMark` (`abu-app/src/components/brand.tsx`) and the web's first paint (`abu-app/public/index.html`) draw the same mark; the splash animation's `MARK_WIDTH` must match `imageWidth`.
  - `design/brand-draft.html` is the earlier draft board (the music book) and the motif.
- **`design/mvp-screens.html`:** the earlier static design board with the brand, palette and screen rationale.

## MVP scope

1. **Accounts and spaces**
   - Sign up; join a group with a 6-letter code; create a group by choosing a type.
   - Group types: Catholic, Anglican, Pentecostal/gospel, band/orchestra. The type only sets default section names and the leader title (Choirmaster, Choir director, Music director). Sections are renamable.
   - Personal space for people without a group.
2. **Song library**
   - Public library with search, tags and filters ("our repertoire", "has sol-fa").
   - Add a song by typing or pasting sol-fa, checked live as it's typed; preview and play before saving.
   - Attach the original sheet (PDF or photo). Songs can exist as sheet only.
3. **Player:** sol-fa score with the current beat highlighted, per-part mute/solo/"mine"/focus, tempo, key (moving doh), loop and practice counts.
4. **Events:** services, concerts and rehearsals, with notes; ordered songs with sections; edit the order (reorder, change section, add, remove); Rehearse and Perform modes as described above.
5. **Attendance:** RSVP, marking by leaders and part leaders, optional self check-in code, and each member's attendance rate.
6. **Group management:** invite code, members grouped by part, change a member's part or role, and notices from leaders and part leaders.
7. **Offline:** saved songs, orders and notices must work without data (church halls). Keep the app light enough for cheap Android phones.

**Later:**
- Pen writing (v1.5).
- **Scanning sheets: Phase 1 is built in the web app** (Songs → Scan, or Add a song → Scan a sheet instead). It's planned in `docs/scan-plan.md`, with Phase 0's measurements in `abu-reader/README.md`.
  - **How it works (owner's rule: the app first, AI as the last resort, Cloudflare's before Anthropic's):** a typed PDF is read from its own text (`abu-app/src/scan/pdf-text.ts`). On photos and scans the lines are found by ink (`page-lines.ts`) and read by SolfaScores' own model in the browser (trained in `abu-reader/train`, served from `abu-app/public/reader/`). Only lines that fail SolfaScores' checker go to Cloudflare's reader (Workers AI, Mistral), then Claude if the Worker has a key. Before reading, the person says whether the sheet is **handwritten or typed** (owner's request, 20 November 2026; Read the sheet waits for the choice): a typed sheet's failing lines go to Haiku, then Opus reads them afresh; a handwritten sheet's skip Haiku and go to Opus (Haiku made 1 of 9 of the owner's handwritten lines add up, Opus all 9), and a handwritten PDF is read from its pictures even if it has text (a scanner's guess at the writing); rows of words in pictures go to Cloudflare's reader (`abu-api/src/scan.ts`, daily allowance in `scan_usage`; a row that's only the notes' names is dropped), but where Opus reads a line, its words for each part are kept instead (it reads handwritten words far better: owner's call, 20 November 2026), and on a handwritten sheet a line that adds up without Opus has Opus copy just its words, given its notes (`/api/scan/line-words`, low effort, about 0.3 to 1 US cent a line; owner's call, 20 November 2026), and so does the first page's heading, for the title, composer, key and time (`/api/scan/heading`); where no time is read it's guessed from the bars (`guessBeats` in `join.ts`). The person checks every line against its picture (`abu-app/src/app/scan.tsx`), and the song editor opens with the original sheet attached.
  - **Accuracy:** with no AI at all, the browser test's typed PDF and photo-like picture each read 48 of 48 bars. Typed PDFs that draw their bar lines (long = bar, short or thin = half-bar) read too: the owner's Handel *Worthy is the Lamb* has 18 of 19 lines adding up (the 19th has two misprints in the sheet). Bridge notes (a small raised note before a note, where the key changes) are read as `s=d`. On the owner's 82 typed sample PDFs, 68.2% of bars (617 of 1,039 lines) pass the checker with no AI: free repairs (`bestFree`/`freeRepair` in `line-text.ts`: left-out beat marks and bar lines, half-bar marks that are bar lines and bar lines that are half-bar marks, parts resting through an upbeat, silent bars typed short), a tall typed `|` read as a bar line, and a second reading with bar lines lined up by where they're printed (`alignBars`, `lined`). An AI's answer is kept only if it passes and still has every part (`keepsParts`): in a trial on 93 failing typed lines, Haiku fixed about 28 correctly at 0.24 US cents a line, but dropped parts on 9 others that still passed; most of its wrong answers came from a line's picture cut wrongly. Measure with the same rules the app uses (pages over half picture are read as pictures), and look at lines a change makes pass: a reading that passes but invents notes or rests is worse than one left to check. On Check the reading, the parts' bars are lined up in columns (`alignedRows` in `line-text.ts`), and each bar line is drawn as one line through the parts' rows, left out across rows of words, as on a sheet (owner's request, 15 November 2026). Bar lines are kept in step across the parts, since they're what the beats are counted and the song arranged by (owner's request, 15 November 2026): on a sheet set in a table, each border in the picture, ruled or drawn by hand (leaning, wobbly or with small gaps, and running through the paper between the rows, so a stack of typed `|` isn't one), is a bar line in every part it runs through, where most of those parts read one (`tableBorders`, `runsThrough` and `barsFromTable` in `rows.ts`; a `|` is made `/` only on a sheet ruled with straight lines, as before); a bar too long by whole bars in every part alike, with a half-bar mark at each cut in most parts, is cut at the same beats in all of them (`withBarsInStep` in `line-text.ts`), even when the line still fails for something else; and a bar line most parts have after a beat is given to a part that lacks it (`withBarLinesInStep`, kept only if the line passes). Check the reading numbers the bars above the rows, marks each bar that doesn't add up with its beats (`bar-check.ts`), and underlines a part whose bars or beats don't match most parts'. On 50 typed test lines, the own model alone gets 95.1% of bars; with Haiku on every line, 99.2% (measured with the earlier model). **Handwriting** (14 November 2026): the model was retrained on 1,041 handwritten rows from choirpdfs.com's public previews, read by Claude Opus (US$6.99 on the owner's trial key, which the owner approved). On handwritten rows from unseen pages, 12.8% of symbols came out wrong (was 36%); retrained on 16 November 2026 on 5,333 more handwritten rows from 252 other preview pages, read by Opus through the Batches API (US$30.20 on the trial key, approved by the owner), it's 10.1% (and 2.8% on typed rows, was 3.3%). Rows of notes are told from rows of words by weights learnt from those pages' rows (`notesRows` in `page-lines.ts`): on 51 hand-labelled pages, 146 of 833 words rows taken for notes (159 by the beat marks alone) and 1,155 of 1,181 rows of notes kept. **Boxed sheets** (20 November 2026, after the owner's *Ekeresimesi Erutego* came out unreadable): most handwritten sheets (41 of the 50 test pages) are tables with one bar of every part in each box, the parts stacked at heights of their own, so they're read box by box (`abu-app/src/scan/boxes.ts`), and kept only if that reads at least 60% of the notes the rows across the page hold (a missed faint rule runs systems together). On the 50 handwritten test pages in the app with no AI, 63 of 303 lines add up (38 of 325 before; 16 pages better, none worse). Opus reads boxed lines well: on the owner's sheet, of the 9 lines still failing after Cloudflare's reader, Opus got all 9 to add up (one after the free repairs, which now run on AI answers too) and the ones checked by eye are right; Haiku got 1 (US$0.24 in all on the trial key). Pages are cut into lines at the ruled lines that box the systems, each line with its own number of parts (`groupLines` in `page-lines.ts`, 16 November 2026, after the owner's *Adam Lay Ybounen* had a three-part semi-chorus that pushed every later line out of place): against 51 pages labelled row by row, 262 of 326 systems are cut exactly (was 51). With the bar lines kept in step (15 November 2026), checked blind against the pictures on the 195 lines it changed, bar lines missed fell from 1,222 to 428 and invented ones rose from 20 to 57 (the new reading closer to the sheet on 179 lines, the old on 9, mostly where the time was guessed wrong); bar columns where every part has the time's beats went from 144 to 218. Details in `abu-reader/README.md`.
