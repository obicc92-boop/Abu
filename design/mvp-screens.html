Eight screens to take a choir or music group off paper

A phone-friendly web app for choirs and music groups: Catholic, Anglican and Pentecostal choirs, and orchestras, bands and other groups outside the church. It starts with tonic sol-fa. The player (screen 4) works: press play to hear the hymn in four parts, then mute, solo or focus on one part, change the tempo, or move doh to another key.

Example content only. The choir, members and songs other than the hymn are made up for this mockup. The hymn is Old Hundredth (Genevan Psalter, 1551; words by William Kethe, 1561), which is in the public domain. The four-part harmony is a simple demo arrangement.

Brand

How the screens connect

Four tabs, each with a short path to its main job.

Home

  1. Next service → its song order
  2. Next rehearsal → I'm going / can't come
  3. Learn your part → player, your part in focus
  4. Notices from the choirmaster

Songs

  1. Search and filter the library
  2. Song → player
  3. New song → sol-fa editor
  4. Save for offline use

Events

  1. Upcoming services, concerts and rehearsals
  2. Event → song order → performance mode
  3. Leader: create an event and order its songs

Group

  1. Invite code and share link
  2. Members by voice part
  3. Roles: Leader (shown as Choirmaster, Music director or Bandleader), Part leader, Member
  4. Profile and my part

Design decisions

Sol-fa stays sol-fa

We don't convert it to staff notation. Each beat gets a column, with “:” between beats and “|” between bars, just like printed sheets. Changing the key changes only the “Doh is …” label, as it does on paper. Tenor is written an octave above where it sounds, as on printed sheets; the app plays it at the right pitch.

Your part comes first

Each member sets their voice part once. The player opens with that part in focus and the others quieter. Home tracks how far you've got with each song.

Built for church halls

Saved songs, orders and notices work without data. Large tap targets, high contrast, and pages that stay small for cheaper Android phones.

One app for every kind of group

When a group is created, its type sets sensible defaults: Mass parts for Catholic choirs, Introit, Psalm and Anthem for Anglican, Praise, Worship and Special number for Pentecostal, and Opening, Sets and Encore for bands and orchestras. Leaders can rename sections and add parts or instruments.

Two kinds of user

Leaders build and organise: songs, events, orders, notices. Members mostly read and listen. Members see no admin controls, so their screens stay simple.

Questions for you

  1. Should the first version include attendance at rehearsals, or leave that for later?
  2. Orchestras and bands often read staff notation rather than sol-fa. Is sol-fa enough for the first non-church groups you have in mind, or do they need staff notation early?
  3. Can you share one or two real sol-fa sheets from your choir, even photos? They'll be the test for the notation format.
